The Problem Space
THE ILLUSION OF PRIVACY

Why Commercial Messengers Fall Short

Commercial platforms trade user privacy for metadata mining, cellular carrier dependence, and centralized key custody.

  • Telegram's Dangerous Default: All standard 1-on-1 and group chats are stored in decrypted form on Telegram servers. The server holds the decryption keys.
  • WhatsApp's Closed Metadata Mining: While Signal protocol encrypts the message body, Meta's closed-source binary mines timestamps, social graphs, IP addresses, and contact books.
  • SIM-Swapping & SS7 Vulnerability: Both platforms bind account identity to phone numbers, making them vulnerable to cellular carrier bribery and SS7 telecom interception.

Pillar 1 · Confidentiality
DATABASE-BLIND ARCHITECTURE

Client-Side Cryptographic Vault

Plaintext messages and unencrypted private keys never touch the network.

Dual-Envelope Asymmetric Encryption

Each message generates an ephemeral AES-256-GCM key in browser RAM, wrapped using the recipient's RSA-OAEP 2048-bit public key.

Client-Wrapped Key Custody

Private keys are encrypted client-side with PBKDF2 (600,000 SHA-256 rounds + 16-byte salt) before upload. Protects confidentiality against a database-only compromise.

Pillar 2 · Integrity
TAMPER-PROOF DATA ASSURANCE

Authenticated Encryption (AEAD)

Detecting and preventing unauthorized data manipulation both in transit and at rest.

  • 128-bit Galois Authentication Tag: AES-256-GCM calculates a cryptographic MAC. Corrupting even 1 single bit in the database triggers an instantaneous decryption rejection.
  • TOTP Replay Protection: The backend increments a monotonic last_counter integer, preventing attackers from replaying captured 6-digit TOTP codes within the 30s window.
  • Constant-Time Verification: Cryptographic comparisons use secrets.compare_digest to defeat timing side-channel attacks.

Pillar 3 · Availability
RESILIENCE & FAULT TOLERANCE

Service Continuity & Disaster Recovery

Protecting central infrastructure from denial of service while guaranteeing user disaster recovery.

Zero-CPU Crypto Offloading

Cryptographic computation is offloaded 100% to client hardware using browser Web Crypto APIs, preventing Cryptographic Denial of Service (Crypto-DoS) on server CPUs.

Atomic Fixed-Window Throttles

Database-backed rate limiters enforce 5-attempt OTP lockouts alongside per-IP and per-account limits, tuned to mitigate brute-force and lockout abuse.

8 Disaster Recovery Codes

If a student loses or destroys their phone, 8 high-entropy one-time recovery codes (stored as SHA-256 digests) guarantee access recovery.

Anti-MITM Verification
SOLVING THE PKI TRUST DILEMMA

60-Digit Cryptographic Safety Numbers

Mathematical defense against Key Substitution Attacks without relying on centralized Certificate Authorities.

  • 1
    The Man-In-The-Middle Threat: In standard PKI, a rogue database administrator could swap Alice's public key with Mallory's key to intercept messages.
  • 2
    Deterministic Pairwise Hashing: DUETCLOCK sorts both students' public keys lexicographically and iteratively hashes them to compute an identical 60-digit number on both devices.
  • 3
    Out-of-Band Attestation: Students verify this 60-digit sequence verbally or in class. If either key was altered by 1 bit, the digits diverge completely.
